This claim is dangerous because custom Solr components often embed critical business logic that becomes invisible during upgrades, migrations, or personnel transitions. Here are the key component types to inventory:

## Critical Custom Components to Document

**Analysis Chain (Tokenization & Filtering)**
- Custom `Analyzer`, `Tokenizer`, `TokenFilter` implementations
- Domain-specific text processing (e.g., product code parsing, chemical nomenclature)
- **Why dangerous**: Search relevance depends entirely on this. Without documentation, you can't migrate to Elasticsearch or upgrade Solr versions without silent query behavior changes.

**Search & Query Handling**
- Custom `QueryParser`, `QParserPlugin`
- Custom `RequestHandler` (beyond standard `/select`, `/update`)
- Custom `ResponseWriter` (beyond JSON/XML)
- **Why dangerous**: Business logic for how queries are parsed/executed is buried here. You don't know what you'd lose if you switched platforms.

**Scoring & Ranking**
- Custom `SimilarityFactory` (TF-IDF customizations)
- Custom scoring plugins
- **Why dangerous**: Affects search quality and user experience in ways you can't reproduce elsewhere.

**Indexing Pipeline**
- Custom `UpdateProcessor` (runs during document indexing)
- Custom field type definitions
- **Why dangerous**: Data transformation logic that affects what's searchable. Losing this means re-indexing or corrupt data.

**Schema & Field Types**
- Non-standard field type definitions in `schema.xml`
- Custom copyField logic
- **Why dangerous**: Core data model assumptions embedded without explanation.

## Why This Claim Is Dangerous

1. **Vendor lock-in**: You're stuck with Solr forever or face a massive re-implementation
2. **Knowledge loss**: When the original author leaves, you have no idea why search works
3. **Silent breakage**: Solr upgrades can break custom components without error messages
4. **Migration cost**: Moving to managed Solr, Elasticsearch, or newer versions becomes 10x more expensive
5. **Compliance gaps**: Undocumented data transformations in custom components may violate audit requirements
6. **Performance blindness**: You don't understand your own system's behavior

**Immediate action**: Audit the Solr installation for anything in `lib/`, custom entries in `solrconfig.xml`, and any non-standard classes. Treat "we don't think we have custom components" as a red flag for missing documentation, not actual absence.
